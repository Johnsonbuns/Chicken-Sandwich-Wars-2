Chicken Power Rankings

The CSW Score answers the question the whole category is built on — who is winning? — using only published figures. Every component comes from a company disclosure, a regulatory filing or an industry data provider. Nothing here is a vibe.

These rankings will not match the ones people expect, and that is the point. Wingstop, the category's development champion, sits mid-table because domestic same-store sales fell 7.5% in Q2 20261. KFC ranks ahead of Popeyes despite closing more than 300 U.S. restaurants2, because its comps have turned positive3 while Popeyes has posted six consecutive negative quarters4.

Dave's is the fastest-growing large chain in America and the single most important development story in chicken real estate. Sales grew 51% and units 52.3% in 2025, Roark bought control at a $1B valuation, and the brand opened eight restaurants in one day in 2026.

Cane's is the only brand in the category compounding double-digit unit growth on top of a $6M+ AUV, and it does it with a four-item menu and no franchisees to manage. That combination — corporate control, extreme menu simplicity, elite volumes — is why it has moved from regional curiosity to the clearest structural threat to Chick-fil-A in a decade.

El Pollo Loco is the category's counter-programming: flame-grilled, Mexican-inspired, and largely insulated from the fried-chicken arms race. It is also the smallest growth story here — nine openings in a year against a 500-unit base, and flat full-year comps of +0.1%.

Slim Chickens has quietly built the largest committed pipeline-to-current-units ratio in the category — more than 1,200 units in development against roughly 300 open. That ratio is the single most useful number for anyone selling sites or capital to this brand, and it is also the number most likely to disappoint: signed area-development agreements convert to open restaurants at well under 100% across franchising generally.

Chicken Salad Chick is the only brand in this database that isn't really in the chicken sandwich war — it is a lunch-daypart, dine-in, non-fried concept with a $1.48M AUV and almost no drive-thru dependency. That makes it a poor comp for the rest of the category and an excellent one for anyone underwriting inline retail: the brand adds units at 13% a year without competing for the pads that Cane's, Chick-fil-A and Dave's are bidding up..

Zaxby's spent the last several years fixing operations rather than chasing unit count, and the payoff is a $2.71M AUV that sits second only to Chick-fil-A and Cane's among franchised chicken chains. Crossing 1,000 units and then walking into the Bronx and the Upper East Side is a genuine test: Zaxby's has never had to make a 2,800 SF, seat-constrained, no-drive-thru box work at Southeast economics.

Wingstop is the sharpest divergence story in the category: development is still exceptional and comps have fallen off a cliff. The brand opened 493 net units in fiscal 2025 (+19.2%) and another 102 in a single quarter of 2026, while domestic same-store sales fell 7.5% in Q2 2026 and management cut full-year comp guidance to -4% to -6%.
