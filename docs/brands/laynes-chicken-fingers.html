CSW Analysis

Layne's doubled its footprint in 2025 and is on track to do it again in 2026. The tell is who is signing: franchisees of Wendy's and Arby's, not first-time owners. When experienced QSR operators put capital into a 40-unit brand, they are underwriting the prototype economics, not the brand equity. Texas is the proving ground — a 44-store single agreement in the home state means the brand's next two years of unit growth are concentrated in a handful of hands, with all the development-speed upside and concentration risk that implies.

Real estate profile

Cap rate
Emerging-brand franchisee credit; effectively no institutional trading history3

At 40–50 open units, Layne's real estate is still being underwritten on operator credit and personal guarantees rather than brand credit. That is normal at this stage and is exactly the window in which a developer can trade capital for below-market rent.
