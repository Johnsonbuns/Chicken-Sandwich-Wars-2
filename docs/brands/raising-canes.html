CSW Analysis

Cane's is the only brand in the category compounding double-digit unit growth on top of a $6M+ AUV, and it does it with a four-item menu and no franchisees to manage. That combination — corporate control, extreme menu simplicity, elite volumes — is why it has moved from regional curiosity to the clearest structural threat to Chick-fil-A in a decade. For real estate, the absence of franchising is the whole story: every Cane's is a corporate credit, so its ground leases price like investment-grade paper even though the company is private. Watch the London flagship. A company-operated international build is a capital-intensive way to grow, and it signals that Graves intends to own the brand's economics abroad rather than sell them to master franchisees.

Real estate profile

Cap rate
4.70%–5.00% on 15-year ground leases (~5.00% asking, Q1 2026)4

Because Cane's builds and operates rather than franchises, its net-lease product carries a corporate guarantee — which is why it prices within 40–60 bps of Chick-fil-A. Standard structure is a 20-year absolute-NNN ground lease with 10% increases every five years. A corporate-guaranteed 15-year ground lease traded at a 4.75% cap on a $6.84M price in a deal arranged by SRS and JLL.45
